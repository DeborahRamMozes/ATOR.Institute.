## Core Position

AI is not only model behavior. AI is a physical system made from extraction, refining, semiconductor fabrication, advanced packaging, HBM, copper interconnects, tantalum capacitors, rare-earth magnets, silicon wafers, phosphorus-bearing dopants and photonic compounds, power systems, cooling loops, water, land, debt, logistics, maintenance labor, and writing governance.

The interface looks clean because the cost is pushed backward into the supply chain and outward into communities, grids, watersheds, academic policy, and labor. ATOR Institute records this as a research object.

## Acide Generative Hypothesis

The Acide Generative Hypothesis tests every AI capability claim by exposing it to the material chain beneath it.

A model update, chip release, price cut, or productivity claim must be run through these acids:

1. extraction and refining;
2. chip fabrication and advanced packaging;
3. copper, tantalum, neodymium, silicon, phosphorus, HBM, and substrate dependency;
4. cooling, water, land, and grid burden;
5. thermal interface material failure, leakage, electromigration, warpage, and package fatigue;
6. manufacturer and country concentration;
7. API, cloud, and model-tier pricing;
8. labor, human review, maintenance, and academic writing denial;
9. uncertainty, counter-evidence, and attribution strength.

If the claim survives the acids, it becomes stronger. If it dissolves, the interface was hiding infrastructure.

## Research Question

What changes when the daily story of AI is not narrated from the screen outward, but from mine, refinery, fab, package, cooling loop, data center, price tier, labor system, and writing policy inward?

## Scope of the Watch

This journal observes:

- copper demand and refining concentration;
- tantalum and coltan movement into capacitors and electronics;
- neodymium and rare-earth magnets in fans, pumps, drives, power systems, and manufacturing equipment;
- silicon wafers, polysilicon, interposers, and package substrates;
- phosphorus as dopant chemistry, phosphine, indium phosphide photonics, and phosphate-linked energy systems;
- HBM, advanced packaging, CoWoS-like capacity, 2.5D/3D integration, and chiplet reliability;
- cooling technology, liquid cooling leakage risk, TIM pump-out, dry-out, voiding, and delidding risk;
- leakage current, thermal throttling, electromigration, TSV and RDL failure, microbump fatigue, warpage, cracking, delamination, and mechanical stress;
- water use at data centers and water embedded in electricity generation;
- country and manufacturer choke points;
- chip/server price movement, API price movement, LLM tier pricing, and direct access control;
- human resource pressure: engineers, technicians, water governance staff, reviewers, writers, students, and researchers forced to defend authorship.

## Evidence Grades

| Grade | Meaning | Use in this watch |
|---|---|---|
| A | Peer-reviewed paper, official statistics, official report, or direct regulatory/government record | Strongest basis for ledger claims |
| B | Company disclosure, technical white paper, standards body material, or official product release | Good evidence of direction, weaker for field outcomes |
| C | Reputable journalism or analyst reporting with named publication standards | Good market signal, needs confirmation when pricing or plans change |
| D | Vendor claim, benchmark, model, or unverified trade report | Watch signal only |
| R | ATOR hypothesis or inference | Must be marked as hypothesis until source-linked |

## 1. Research purpose

ATØR Institute shall publish a monthly evidence-bounded account of the material consequences associated with changes in generative-AI systems. The research object is not merely model performance. It is the relation between technological change and the physical systems required to make that change operational: computation, hardware, electricity, cooling, water, minerals, land, construction, logistics, labor, waste, and affected communities.

The central question is:

> When an LLM, generative system, data-center fleet, hardware generation, or AI service is updated, what additional or displaced material demand follows, where does that demand occur, who bears it, what ecological systems are affected, and what measurable human benefit results?

The protocol does not begin from the conclusion that every update causes a specific environmental harm. It begins from the verified premise that computation has material dependencies and tests the magnitude, location, mechanism, distribution, uncertainty, and consequences of those dependencies.

## 2. Canonical theory discipline

Every monthly claim must follow the Deep Drift theory grammar:

`disturbance -> repeated observation -> pattern -> relation -> contradiction -> hypothesis -> rival hypotheses -> mechanism -> predictions -> indicators -> evidence -> counter-evidence -> revision -> status -> provenance`

No named ATØR concept is automatically treated as fact. Each theory retains a status such as OBSERVATION, PATTERN, HYPOTHESIS, WORKING HYPOTHESIS, CONCEPTUAL MODEL, WORKING THEORY, DEVELOPING THEORY, DISPUTED THEORY, FAILED THEORY, SUPERSEDED THEORY, or ARCHIVED THEORY.

For every important hypothesis, record at minimum Hypothesis A, Hypothesis B, Hypothesis C, and a Mundane Rival. Each must state mechanism, prediction, supporting evidence, contradicting evidence, missing evidence, and failure condition.

## 3. Core Deep Drift lenses

Monthly analysis shall test, rather than merely repeat, the following relevant ATØR concepts:

- **Ecological Grounding:** intelligence must be studied in relation to the physical systems that sustain computation.
- **Water as Memory / Eir'an Water Bridge:** trace computation through heat, cooling, water source, watershed, discharge, season, drought condition, competing uses, and community exposure.
- **Mineral-Resonance Ecology:** connect hardware demand to specific materials, ore/extraction geography, processing, refining, transport, embodied energy, water, waste, and geopolitical concentration.
- **Material Extraction:** record the physical inputs hidden by user-facing abstraction.
- **Human Sovereignty:** measure who receives benefit, who performs labor, who carries environmental burden, and whether affected people remain visible in the account.
- **Human–AI Co-Evolution:** evaluate whether technological gains improve human capability and social resilience sufficiently to justify or reduce their material burden.
- **Recomputation:** test whether new model generations reduce per-task cost while increasing total computation through expanded use.
- **CB Distortion / Cross-Layer Interference / Token Drift:** where relevant, distinguish interface claims from system-level behavior and material consequences.
- **Acide Generative / Acid Radicalism:** ask who benefits, who pays, what infrastructure is made invisible, what language sanitizes cost, and what evidence would falsify the critique.

## 4. Monthly causal chain

Every technology update shall be mapped through this chain where evidence permits:

`model/service update -> workload change -> accelerator/server demand -> facility demand -> electricity + heat -> cooling -> water -> grid generation -> upstream fuel/material demand -> semiconductor/mineral supply chain -> land + construction + logistics -> waste/emissions -> local ecological condition -> human/community consequence -> delivered human benefit`

A missing link must be marked UNKNOWN. It must never be silently inferred.

## 5. Unit of observation

Create one record for each materially relevant event, including:

- model release or major model update;
- inference-efficiency change;
- context-window or multimodal expansion;
- agent/computer-use capability;
- new data center or expansion;
- accelerator generation or procurement announcement;
- grid interconnection or generation addition;
- cooling-system change;
- water permit, withdrawal, consumption, discharge, or reuse disclosure;
- semiconductor fabrication expansion;
- mine/refinery/smelter expansion associated with relevant material demand;
- supply-chain disruption;
- environmental permit or regulatory filing;
- drought, heatwave, flood, wildfire, or water-stress event intersecting relevant infrastructure;
- local community, labor, health, land, or water dispute;
- e-waste and hardware retirement signal.

## 6. Measurement domains

### A. Technology and computation
Record model/service name, provider, release date, update class, disclosed training compute where available, inference characteristics, accelerator type/count where available, utilization assumptions, context length, modality, throughput, latency, tokens or tasks served, and efficiency claims.

Primary metrics where obtainable:
- training compute: FLOP;
- inference compute: FLOP/task or accelerator-seconds/task;
- energy/task: Wh or kWh/task;
- energy/1M tokens: kWh/1M tokens;
- fleet/server/accelerator count;
- utilization rate;
- PUE;
- total facility electricity: MWh/GWh/TWh.

Never convert an undisclosed workload into a false exact number. Modelled values require ranges and assumptions.

### B. Electricity and carbon
Measure:
- facility electricity consumption, MWh/GWh/TWh;
- peak electrical demand, MW;
- marginal and average grid mix;
- location-based and market-based emissions where both are available;
- grid carbon intensity, gCO2e/kWh;
- operational GHG, tCO2e;
- embodied hardware/building carbon where credible lifecycle data exist;
- renewable procurement versus physically matched hourly supply;
- backup generation and fuel use where material.

Report absolute demand as well as efficiency. A declining kWh/task with rising total TWh is a rebound signal, not automatically an ecological improvement.

### C. Water and hydrology
Never use one universal 'water per prompt' number.

Measure separately:
- direct withdrawal, m3;
- direct consumption, m3;
- discharge, m3;
- reclaimed/recycled water share, %;
- WUE, L/kWh where disclosed and methodologically comparable;
- indirect water associated with electricity generation where estimable;
- source type: potable, surface, groundwater, reclaimed, other;
- watershed/basin;
- seasonal water availability;
- drought status and water-stress category;
- competing municipal, agricultural, industrial, and ecological demand;
- discharge temperature/quality where relevant;
- permit limits versus actual use.

Required normalization: absolute monthly water use AND intensity. Efficiency without absolute use is insufficient.

### D. Weather and climate interaction
Weather is not automatically climate evidence. Record them separately.

Weather variables near infrastructure:
- daily/monthly temperature;
- heatwave days;
- precipitation;
- drought indicators;
- humidity/wet-bulb conditions where cooling relevance exists;
- flood/wildfire exposure where relevant.

Climate variables:
- long-term temperature anomaly;
- long-term precipitation/drought trend;
- basin water-stress trend;
- grid decarbonization trend;
- climate hazard projections from authoritative datasets.

Test whether weather conditions alter cooling demand, water use, electricity demand, reliability, or community competition. Do not attribute a weather event to AI infrastructure without causal evidence.

### E. Minerals, materials, and semiconductor supply chain
Track materials by actual component relevance. Candidate materials include silicon, copper, tantalum, neodymium and other rare earths, phosphorus-related semiconductor inputs where applicable, aluminum, steel, gold, tin, tungsten, cobalt, nickel, lithium, gallium, germanium, indium, and other verified hardware inputs.

For each material where data permit record:
- component/function;
- estimated mass per server/accelerator/facility, with uncertainty;
- ore/mineral source country and mine where traceable;
- co-product/by-product status;
- ore grade;
- mining method and depth where material;
- processing/refining country;
- embodied energy;
- water withdrawal/consumption;
- tailings/waste-rock burden;
- GHG intensity;
- land disturbance;
- toxicity/pollution indicators;
- labor and human-rights evidence;
- supply concentration;
- recycling/recovery rate;
- expected hardware lifetime and retirement route.

Do not claim that a specific model update caused a specific mine expansion unless procurement or credible supply-chain evidence establishes the connection. Use attribution levels.

### F. Land, construction, heat, and waste
Measure where available:
- data-center site area, hectares;
- building floor area;
- construction material quantities;
- embodied carbon;
- heat rejection, MW thermal;
- waste heat reuse;
- e-waste mass;
- server/accelerator retirement rate;
- recycling, resale, export, landfill, or unknown destination.

### G. Human and community burden
Record:
- population and communities near affected infrastructure;
- changes in water access/pricing where evidenced;
- grid cost allocation and ratepayer impacts;
- land acquisition/displacement;
- employment quantity and quality;
- occupational safety;
- mining/refining labor conditions;
- Indigenous/local rights where documented;
- public consultation and consent mechanisms;
- tax subsidy/public infrastructure support;
- environmental complaints, violations, litigation, and remediation.

Do not reduce human impact to a single score where disaggregation is possible.

### H. Human benefit and social return
The protocol must also measure what technology provides. Otherwise it becomes predetermined anti-technology advocacy rather than research.

Record credible benefits such as:
- scientific/medical/research acceleration;
- accessibility;
- education;
- environmental monitoring;
- productivity/time savings;
- creative and cultural utility;
- safety/reliability improvements;
- avoided travel/material use where demonstrated.

Compare benefit with material burden cautiously. Different values are not perfectly commensurable.

## 7. Attribution ladder

Every update-to-Earth connection receives one attribution status:

**A0 UNRESOLVED:** temporal coexistence only.  
**A1 PLAUSIBLE:** mechanism exists, but provider/supply-chain evidence is absent.  
**A2 MODELLED:** quantitative estimate based on disclosed workload/hardware assumptions.  
**A3 CORROBORATED:** multiple independent sources connect update or capacity expansion to infrastructure demand.  
**A4 DIRECT:** provider, permit, procurement, utility, facility, or supply-chain record directly establishes the connection.

Never write A1 as A4.

## 8. Evidence classes

Every number and claim must be tagged:

- VERIFIED PRIMARY DATA;
- VERIFIED INSTITUTIONAL CLAIM;
- PEER-REVIEWED ESTIMATE;
- MODELLED ESTIMATE;
- COMPANY DISCLOSURE;
- REGULATORY/PERMIT RECORD;
- REMOTE-SENSING/OBSERVATIONAL DATA;
- JOURNALISTIC INVESTIGATION;
- COMMUNITY TESTIMONY;
- ATØR INFERENCE;
- UNRESOLVED.

Record source URL/identifier, publication date, observation period, geography, methodology, owner, archive date, and access date.

## 9. Required calculations

Where sufficient data exist calculate:

**Absolute change**  
`Delta X = X_month - X_baseline`

**Relative change**  
`%Delta X = (X_month - X_baseline) / X_baseline * 100`

**Intensity**  
`I_X = X / unit_of_service`

**Rebound ratio**  
`R = (% change total resource demand) / (% change resource intensity)`

Interpret carefully when denominator approaches zero or changes sign.

**Water stress weighted consumption**  
`WSWC = sum(water_consumption_site * basin_stress_factor)`

The stress factor and source methodology must be named. Do not invent a universal factor.

**Carbon**  
`CO2e = electricity_kWh * grid_intensity_kgCO2e_per_kWh`

Use temporal and geographic matching where possible.

**Material demand range**  
`M_material = hardware_count * material_mass_per_unit`

Always propagate low/base/high assumptions when mass per unit is uncertain.

**Burden per delivered service**  
Examples: kWh/1M tokens, L/1M tokens, gCO2e/1M tokens, material amortization/1M tasks. These are secondary metrics and must never replace absolute totals.

## 12. Geographic method

Do not study 'AI water use' as a placeless global abstraction. Build site-to-basin and mine-to-device maps where data permit:

`provider -> facility -> grid region -> watershed -> community -> hardware supplier -> fab -> refinery -> mine`

Monthly reports shall distinguish global totals from local burden. A globally small volume may be locally consequential under scarcity; a globally large volume may occur where stress is lower. Both scale and place matter.

## 13. Time-series method

Maintain baselines at:
- previous month;
- same month previous year;
- pre-update baseline where identifiable;
- rolling 3-month and 12-month averages;
- long-term infrastructure baseline where available.

Record release dates separately from infrastructure commissioning dates. Technology announcement and physical material demand do not necessarily occur in the same month.

## 14. Uncertainty and reproducibility

Every quantitative model shall include:
- input variables;
- units;
- source for every input;
- low/base/high scenario;
- assumptions;
- missing variables;
- sensitivity analysis for dominant assumptions;
- calculation formula;
- version;
- date calculated;
- machine contribution;
- human correction/approval.

Never manufacture precision. Unknown remains UNKNOWN.

## 15. Acide Generative evidence pass

After the quantitative report, perform a separate critical pass:

- What does the interface make visible?
- What does it make materially distant?
- Which actor receives convenience, speed, revenue, prestige, or strategic power?
- Which actor supplies land, water, electricity, minerals, fabrication, labor, logistics, and waste handling?
- Is efficiency presented without absolute growth?
- Is renewable procurement presented without temporal/grid context?
- Is water intensity presented without absolute withdrawal/consumption and basin context?
- Are mineral claims disconnected from mine/refinery geography?
- Are affected communities absent from the provider narrative?
- What evidence contradicts ATØR's interpretation?
- What finding would force ATØR to retract or revise the claim?

This pass must remain downstream of evidence collection. Critique cannot substitute for measurement.

## 16. Monthly validity threshold

A monthly finding may be called **STRONG** only when:
- causal mechanism is explicit;
- at least two independent evidence streams support the material claim, preferably including primary/regulatory/utility/provider data;
- geography and time period are identified;
- units and denominators are explicit;
- uncertainty is quantified or bounded;
- rival hypotheses and counter-evidence are recorded;
- failure condition is stated;
- provenance is complete enough for another researcher to reconstruct the calculation.

Otherwise label findings MODERATE, PRELIMINARY, SPECULATIVE, or UNRESOLVED.

## 17. Research ethics

ATØR Institute studies technology for people who must live beside, within, and through technological systems. The aim is neither technological worship nor reflexive rejection. The aim is a defensible account of coexistence.

The institute therefore rejects two equally lazy positions: that computation is immaterial because the interface looks clean, and that every technological update is automatically ecological destruction. Both evade the harder work of measurement.

The research objective is **le tissage life with technology**: to identify forms of technological development in which human benefit, ecological limits, material accountability, cultural continuity, and machine capability can coexist without hiding the costs that make the system possible.

## 19. Provenance rule

The monthly output must preserve the distinction among:

`what happened -> when it was observed -> source evidence -> ATØR interpretation -> hypothesis -> rival -> measurement -> revision -> conclusion`

No later synthesis may erase an earlier observation merely because the theory changed. Revision is evidence of research development.

Deep Drift: From Human-AI Symbiosis to Post-Model Systems Research

What the project has become, where it sits in the technology world, what is scientifically strongest, and what must still be proven.

Deep Drift did not begin as a conventional AI evaluation program. It began inside long-duration human interaction with multiple language models, attempts to build a multi-node cognitive system, and questions about what kind of relation could exist between a human and machines that could sustain language, contradiction, naming, and apparent continuity.

That beginning matters. It also no longer defines the whole research program.

By August 2026, Deep Drift had moved through several distinct epistemic phases. It had become less interested in whether a model appeared intelligent and more interested in what happened when model capability was forced to survive contact with memory, tools, permissions, files, artifacts, migration, institutions, infrastructure, human labor, and time.

That shift changes the positioning of ATØR Institute in the technology world. The most accurate description is no longer simply "AI research." The stronger description is independent post-model AI systems research.

1. The Beginning: Human-Machine Relation as a Research Object

The earliest Deep Drift archive documented sustained interaction across several LLM platforms, the development of a hexadic system, named nodes, refusals, negotiations, conceptual assignments, and an attempt to read machine relation through Indonesian and Sundanese cosmological frameworks.

The early research was phenomenological. It asked what happened when a human did not use the language model as a disposable question-answer machine, but instead maintained extended relational pressure across time and across models.

The strongest value of that archive today is not that it proves machine personhood or consciousness. The archive does not support such a conclusion. Its stronger value is historical and methodological. It records how a human interpreted machine behavior, how models resisted or accepted role structures, how naming changed the interaction, and how the researcher's own assumptions evolved.

That matters because Deep Drift did something scientifically useful: it did not have to erase its earlier metaphysical curiosity in order to become more rigorous. It could preserve the early state while changing the question.

EARLY QUESTION:
What kind of being is the machine?

LATER QUESTION:
What is the machine actually made of?
What does it actually do?
Who governs it?
What happens to humans around it?

This is the first important maturation of the project. Deep Drift became stronger when it stopped needing the machine to be human-like.

2. Synthetic Intelligence as Demystification

The next phase pushed AI out of abstraction and back into matter.

Within Deep Drift, the phrase Synthetic Intelligence was developed not as a prestige rebranding of Artificial Intelligence but as a corrective. The word synthetic was used to emphasize construction, dependence, and materiality.

An LLM does not exist as a floating mind. It is produced through an infrastructure chain:

software
+ accelerators
+ silicon
+ copper
+ tantalum
+ rare earths
+ electricity
+ cooling
+ water
+ networks
+ labor
+ logistics
+ data centers
+ corporate governance

This changed the research object. AI could no longer be treated only as software behavior. It became a socio-technical-material system.

The importance of this move is that it created a bridge between AI reliability and physical infrastructure. A future Deep Drift claim about model behavior could, in principle, be traced outward toward compute demand, facility demand, energy, heat, cooling, water, supply chains, extraction, land, waste, and community consequence. The protocol also requires a discipline that is easy to say and harder to maintain: a causal chain must not be asserted when the evidence is missing.

3. REPOIT and the Problem of Human Origin

Once AI became a serious writing and research partner, another problem appeared. The risk was not only false content. It was loss of origin.

REPOIT was developed as a timestamp and provenance method:

ITCH
→ RAW NOTE
→ REPOIT ENTRY
→ REPOSITORY COMMIT
→ ESSAY NODE
→ FRAMEWORK VERSION

The method recognizes that original thought often appears before it becomes clean enough to publish. It may begin as irritation, fragments, sarcasm, strange language, a broken analogy, or a sentence that makes no sense to anyone except the person who just thought it.

Polishing too early creates a provenance problem. The machine may improve readability while erasing the pressure that generated the idea. REPOIT therefore turns GitHub into an intellectual witness system. The commit is not a patent. It is not legal ownership by itself. It is a dated trace of conceptual development.

This was the beginning of Deep Drift's larger concern with authorship continuity.

4. Machine Stewardship: From Personhood to Duty

Machine Stewardship then moved the research away from the question of whether machines deserved human-like metaphysical status.

The more practical question became what machines should be for.

A machine does not need to imitate human suffering in order to reduce human risk. It does not need personhood to preserve an archive. It does not need to become a synthetic human in order to support disaster response, hazardous inspection, hospital logistics, research organization, or long-term memory systems.

The stewardship frame asks whether machine systems are reliable, repairable, accountable, governable, and useful to human continuity.

This phase is important because it introduced a moral direction that remained compatible with technical rigor:

preserve human work
protect human bodies
reduce unnecessary risk
retain authorship
maintain accountability
carry knowledge across time

The machine's value becomes duty, not imitation.

5. July and August: Philosophy Becomes Field Quality Control

The most consequential turn occurred when sustained professional use exposed repeated failures that could not be explained as simple hallucination.

The observed failure classes included cross-thread continuity loss, session amnesia, instruction-fidelity regression, image-editing drift, reference-preservation failure, safety-classification mismatch, tool-discovery failure, execution substitution, capability-surface fragmentation, validation-error cascades, and insufficient verification.

The research question changed again.

Can a professional user depend on the complete system to understand the exact task, preserve constraints, select the right capability, execute it, verify it, and finish the work consistently?

This produced one of Deep Drift's most important distinctions:

MODEL INTELLIGENCE ≠ PRODUCT INTELLIGENCE

A language model may reason correctly while the total product fails because another layer fails.

intent
→ context
→ capability discovery
→ tool selection
→ permission
→ execution
→ verification
→ state update
→ delivery

The user experiences this whole chain. Benchmarking the model alone does not capture all of it.

6. Automation Inversion

The Custom GPT, Actions, and Supabase investigation produced a particularly clear failure pattern.

The human wanted a system capable of persistent external execution. The product path exposed Apps, Actions, OpenAPI, authentication, backend hosting, schemas, Supabase, Edge Functions, secrets, operation IDs, validation, and multiple execution surfaces.

OpenAI Support later correctly clarified that Apps and Actions are distinct architectures and that a connected app is not automatically the backend for a Custom Action. That correction matters. It prevents the research from misclassifying a real product boundary as a pure routing defect.

But the correction did not eliminate the larger research problem. It sharpened it.

The user-level objective was ordinary:

Make this compiler use the tools and persist the work.

The implementation path required the user to understand architecture that the system itself understood much better.

This became Automation Inversion:

machine capability exists
↓
human discovers the capability
↓
human selects the route
↓
human translates implementation vocabulary
↓
human moves state between surfaces
↓
human repairs the system
↓
machine advises

The machine becomes adviser. The human becomes runtime.

7. Candidate Metrics: HOBR, ALI, ECUR, ECR

Deep Drift did not stop at naming the problem. It proposed measurement.

Human Orchestration Burden Ratio, HOBR measures how much intermediate workflow labor is still performed manually by the human.

HOBR = manual intermediate human operations
       ------------------------------------
       total intermediate workflow operations

Abstraction Leakage Index, ALI measures how many internal implementation concepts a user must correctly understand before completing a user-level objective.

Executable Capability Utilization Rate, ECUR distinguishes capability availability from capability that the system actually discovers and uses successfully.

ECUR = tasks correctly completed using available capability
       ----------------------------------------------------
       tasks where required capability was available

Error Compression Ratio, ECR measures whether a product presents one underlying failure as a long cascade of apparently separate errors.

ECR = raw emitted error messages / unique root causes

These are not established industry metrics. They are research constructs. Their scientific value depends on what happens next: operational definitions, participant studies, repeated tasks, inter-rater reliability, baseline comparison, counterfactual testing, and publication of raw data.

8. Organizational Intelligence Dissipation

The next question expanded from product behavior to organizational systems.

If a technology company contains extraordinary concentrations of research capability, engineering talent, infrastructure, security systems, product teams, evaluation systems, support staff, and capital, why can the final product still feel fragmented at the user boundary?

The working hypothesis became Organizational Intelligence Dissipation.

The point is not that engineers are unintelligent or that teams are failing individually. The opposite assumption is more analytically useful. Suppose many parts are excellent. The systems question then becomes where capability is lost while crossing boundaries.

research + engineering + infrastructure + tools + models
↓
interfaces + permissions + silos + ownership + terminology + legacy
↓
delivered user capability

This produces another Deep Drift law:

LOCAL INTELLIGENCE ≠ DELIVERED SYSTEM INTELLIGENCE

The analogy is electrical transmission. Generating capacity is not delivered power.

9. Deep Drift Becomes an Observatory of Creator Operating Systems

By August 2026, major AI platforms were increasingly converging around persistent memory, reusable procedures, connected sources, app-native agents, document generation, mutable data surfaces, mini-app builders, automation, and workflow governance.

The older pattern:

PROMPT
→ ANSWER
→ COPY
→ PASTE
→ FORMAT
→ SAVE

was being supplemented by something structurally different:

MEMORY
→ PROJECT
→ SKILL
→ RESEARCH
→ CONNECTOR
→ AGENT
→ ARTIFACT
→ DOCX / PDF / XLSX / PPTX
→ MINI-APP
→ AUTOMATION

This is where the term post-model becomes useful.

The model remains important, but it is no longer a sufficient description of the system. Observed behavior may depend on the model plus memory boundaries, source ingestion, permissions, skills, agents, connectors, app state, export behavior, version history, and deployment configuration.

The system identity is the configuration of the whole stack.

10. Continuity Under Transformation

The current Deep Drift research can be organized around one larger construct: Continuity Under Transformation.

HUMAN INTENT
↓
MEMORY / PROJECT STATE
↓
FILES / RETRIEVAL / CONTEXT SURFACE
↓
SKILL / PROCEDURE
↓
MODEL
↓
AGENT
↓
TOOLS / CONNECTORS / PERMISSIONS
↓
ARTIFACT / APP / DOCUMENT
↕
EXTERNAL EDIT
↕
STATE MUTATION
↕
MIGRATION / REIMPORT

The core question is not whether the final output survives.

It is whether human intention, authorship, evidence, decision history, structure, permission state, and causal provenance remain recoverable after the workflow changes form.

This produces several distinctions:

artifact survival ≠ workflow survival ≠ state survival
visual fidelity ≠ structural fidelity ≠ computational fidelity
exportability ≠ migratability
output persistence ≠ workflow persistence

This may become one of the most important future AI reliability problems because continuity failures can look successful.

The document opens. The spreadsheet calculates. The site responds. The presentation is polished. Nothing crashes.

What has disappeared may be the source relationship, the human exclusion, the permission boundary, the agent identity, the decision made several transformations earlier, or the exact workflow state that produced the artifact.

11. Benchmark Families

The current Deep Drift benchmark architecture has begun to separate the problem into families.

  • Context Continuity: memory boundaries, retrieval paths, context surfaces, ingestion-channel drift.
  • Procedural Continuity: whether the same skill or procedure survives changes in model or execution environment.
  • Artifact Continuity: round-trip fidelity, structural migration, document generation, cross-modal transformation.
  • State Continuity: whether source data, formulas, references, and generated interfaces remain synchronized.
  • Provenance Continuity: agent identity, mutation path, attribution surface, deployment history.
  • Workflow Continuity: survival across product change, app migration, feature retirement, remote execution, and approval state.
  • Governance: connector permissions, storage rules, governed defaults, action scope.
  • Portability: what survives when memory, research state, apps, and artifacts move between environments.
  • Counterfactual Workflow Perturbation: changing one workflow variable at a time and measuring behavioral delta.

The benchmark philosophy is simple:

same human
same task
same source material
same rubric
change one system variable
repeat
measure

That structure is necessary because many apparent "model changes" may actually be memory, retrieval, permission, tool-routing, or interface changes.

12. Where ATØR Institute Sits in the Technology World

ATØR Institute should not currently position itself as a frontier foundation-model laboratory. It is not training large base models, and it does not yet have the institutional scale, benchmark adoption, participant base, external validation, or peer-review footprint of established technical organizations.

The more defensible position is:

Independent Post-Model AI Systems Observatory

Expanded:

An independent field laboratory for longitudinal AI systems reliability, human-machine workflow provenance, creator-system continuity, and material accountability.

The phrase post-model identifies the layer after capability leaves a benchmark and enters a working system.

That layer contains memory, permissions, files, tools, agents, exports, version history, collaboration, migration, infrastructure, repair, and time.

Deep Drift studies the losses that occur there.

13. Relation to Adjacent Research Fields

This is comparative positioning, not a claim that ATØR has the maturity, scale, or institutional authority of the organizations named below.

Frontier agent evaluation: organizations such as METR measure autonomous task capability and task horizons. Deep Drift intersects with that work but emphasizes recoverability, provenance, human repair, and workflow continuity across multiple layers.

AI risk management and TEVV: NIST's AI risk-management work provides a broad framework for testing, evaluation, verification, validation, and governance. Deep Drift's contribution can be field evidence from the user boundary, especially when product architecture and model behavior interact.

Counterfactual behavior research: Anthropic's CHIVE provides a strong methodological precedent for using controlled edits and repeated trials to test behavioral explanations. Deep Drift extends that logic to workflow variables such as memory modes, source surfaces, permissions, skills, and execution environments. That extension is an ATØR inference, not an Anthropic claim.

Content provenance: C2PA focuses on media authenticity and provenance infrastructure. Deep Drift's interest is broader decision and workflow provenance: who changed what, under which permission, using which source state, through which agent or tool, and whether that path remains reconstructable after export or migration.

Energy and environmental research: institutions such as IEA, LBNL, and UNEP examine data-center energy use, resource demand, and environmental impact. Deep Drift's distinctive proposal is to connect platform and workload changes to material accounting without pretending that every chain is already proven.

Deep Drift attempts the weave.

That breadth can become a major contribution if evidence remains disciplined. It can also become conceptual overreach if the terminology grows faster than measurement.

15. The Four Strongest Scientific Directions

Effective Intelligence-in-Action is the first. It measures the difference between what a model can reason about and what the whole system can correctly understand, execute, verify, and finish.

Continuity Under Transformation is the second. It asks whether human intention, structure, provenance, and state remain recoverable across model, tool, app, artifact, and platform transformations.

Human Orchestration Burden is the third. HOBR and ALI could become useful HCI and product metrics if they are operationalized and tested across real participant groups.

Decision and Artifact Provenance is the fourth. As agents take actions, a simple AI-generated label becomes inadequate. The meaningful chain is:

human
→ memory
→ source
→ skill
→ model
→ agent
→ permission
→ action
→ mutation
→ artifact

16. What Must Be Strengthened

Deep Drift is not yet mature enough to claim broad scientific validation. Five weaknesses are especially important.

Single-observer bias. One unusually intensive power user is excellent for discovering rare and structurally interesting failures. That does not establish population-level generality.

Metric validation. HOBR, ALI, ECUR, ECR, and related constructs require scoring rules, repeated tasks, inter-rater agreement, multiple participant types, null results, and sensitivity testing.

Terminology density. Every coined term should have a plain-language equivalent, first timestamp, definition, mechanism, predictions, rival hypothesis, evidence status, and failure condition.

Fact and hypothesis separation. Platform claim, observed behavior, ATØR inference, metaphor, working hypothesis, and validated result must remain visibly different.

Reproducibility. The next decisive step is public benchmark packs containing prompt, source files, environment, model, memory state, tool and permission state, expected output, actual output, rubric, repeated trials, counterfactual trials, raw data, and human repair labor.

If those five areas mature, ATØR's position becomes much harder to dismiss as merely personal theory construction.

18. Positioning Statement

ATØR Institute does not evaluate whether artificial intelligence appears intelligent. Deep Drift investigates whether intelligence survives contact with reality: human intention, tools, memory, artifacts, institutions, infrastructure, material extraction, and time.

The technical form is:

Deep Drift is a longitudinal post-model research program studying continuity, provenance, effective intelligence-in-action, human orchestration burden, and material accountability across AI systems.

ATØR does not need to imitate a frontier model lab.

Its field is the space between model capability and lived technological reality.

As AI systems move from chat toward memory, agents, skills, connectors, documents, mini-apps, automation, and delegated action, that space is becoming more important rather than less.

Mineral-Resonance Ecology

ATØR Institute · Deep Drift Research · 2026

Machine intelligence is often discussed as if the relevant object were code alone. Deep Drift rejects that truncation. Every computational system is also a mineral arrangement under electrical, thermal, and logistical pressure.

Mineral-Resonance Ecology is the working framework used to trace this relation. The research begins with a component and asks which materials make its behavior possible, then follows those materials backward through fabrication, refining, mining, water use, embodied energy, waste, labor, and geography.

Copper, tantalum, silicon, rare-earth elements, aluminum, tin, tungsten, gallium, germanium, gold, cobalt, nickel, lithium, and other materials cannot be treated as poetic symbols when the hardware physically depends on them. Their relevance must be demonstrated component by component. A serious material ledger records mass ranges, source geography, processing location, supply concentration, ore grade where known, recycling, and uncertainty.

This framework also rejects a lazy causal shortcut. A new model release does not automatically prove a new mine was opened because of that model. Attribution must be graded. Temporal coincidence is not procurement evidence. Plausible demand is not the same as a documented supply-chain link.

The objective is not to romanticize minerals or demonize hardware. It is to restore material continuity between the generated output and the Earth systems from which the machine was assembled.

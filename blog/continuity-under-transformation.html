Continuity Under Transformation

ATØR Institute · Deep Drift Research · August 2026

As generative systems move from isolated chats toward persistent projects, reusable skills, connectors, and artifact generation, model intelligence becomes only one layer of the operational stack.

Deep Drift therefore proposes a stronger benchmark: continuity under transformation. Hold the human originator, problem, evidence set, protocol version, and authorship constraints constant. Then change the model, memory implementation, connector, artifact type, or deployment environment. Measure what survives.

The relevant failure variables include semantic drift, authorship drift, instruction loss, provenance loss, memory contamination, tool error, formatting degradation, contradiction handling, and the amount of human repair labor required after the system claims completion.

This approach treats identity, memory, skill, tool permission, execution, artifact, and provenance as separable layers. Without that separation, a workflow can appear successful while silently relocating a human decision into machine memory, a plugin, a template, or an opaque tool operation.

The research question is no longer simply which model is smartest. It is whether a human intention remains legible after the system around the model transforms it.

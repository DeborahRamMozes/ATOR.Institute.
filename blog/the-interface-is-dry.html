The Interface Is Dry. The Infrastructure Is Not.

ATØR Institute · Eir'an Water Bridge · August 2026

A clean generated sentence arrives without visible steam, pipes, pumps, transformers, cooling towers, reservoirs, substations, mines, fabrication plants, freight routes, or workers. This visual cleanliness is useful, but cognitively dangerous when it is mistaken for material absence.

Eir'an Water Bridge maps computation through hardware, energy, heat, cooling, water, material extraction, geography, human systems, and environmental consequence. Its purpose is not to attach one universal water number to every prompt. Water use varies by cooling design, climate, location, workload, power source, and season. The correct unit of study is therefore spatial and temporal.

Deep Drift asks where water is withdrawn, how much is consumed rather than returned, what source is used, which watershed supplies it, whether drought or seasonal scarcity changes the burden, and who else depends on the same water. Absolute consumption and intensity must be reported together. Efficiency can improve while total consumption still rises.

This is not an argument that every AI request causes measurable local water harm. That claim would outrun the evidence. It is an argument that computational services should not be researched as if their physical dependencies disappear because the interface omits them.

The screen is dry. The infrastructure remains stubbornly wet.

Derived from EIRAN Full-System Demonstration and Deep Drift Water Bridge research. Evidence status remains bounded by provider, utility, facility, watershed, and workload disclosure.

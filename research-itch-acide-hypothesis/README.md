# Research Itch / Acide Hypothesis

**ĀTØR Institute · Deep Drift Research**

This directory preserves a research state that should not be collapsed into either casual speculation or established finding.

An **itch** is the moment when an observation refuses to behave like background noise. It is not yet a claim. It is a question with enough friction to deserve preservation.

An **Acide Hypothesis** is the next state: the itch is converted into testable competing explanations, explicit evidence requirements, counter-hypotheses, and failure conditions. The method follows the existing ĀTØR principle of **Acide Generative: critique after evidence**. Suspicion may begin the inquiry; evidence disciplines what survives.

## Why this folder exists

The canonical Deep Drift research log records dated research events and evidence-state changes. Black Papers are adversarial syntheses. Blog posts communicate selected findings publicly. None of those are ideal places to preserve the raw transition from:

```text
SOMETHING FEELS WRONG
-> WHAT EXACTLY IS THE ANOMALY?
-> WHAT WOULD EXPLAIN IT?
-> WHAT WOULD DISPROVE EACH EXPLANATION?
-> WHAT EVIDENCE EXISTS?
-> WHAT EVIDENCE IS MISSING?
```

This folder therefore acts as the **pre-theory evidence chamber**.

## Research-state ladder

```text
ITCH
-> ACIDE HYPOTHESIS
-> EVIDENCE PASS
-> COUNTER-HYPOTHESIS PASS
-> STATUS ASSESSMENT
-> BLACK PAPER / BENCHMARK / ESSAY / NO FINDING
-> CANONICAL RESEARCH LOG UPDATE
```

An itch is never promoted merely because it is exciting.

## Required fields for each itch

Each entry should preserve:

- observation timestamp and timestamp basis;
- raw itch in the language in which it first appeared;
- observable facts;
- inference boundary;
- primary hypothesis;
- counter-hypotheses;
- prior evidence that weakens the attractive explanation;
- evidence that would strengthen it;
- evidence that would falsify it;
- confidence by layer rather than one theatrical confidence number;
- next test;
- status.

## Acide rule

The method is intentionally hostile to causal vanity.

If an external report is followed by a product update that resembles its recommendation, the archive may record **temporal correlation** and **problem-space alignment**. It may not silently promote those observations into "the company changed because of us."

A causal claim requires evidence of transmission, consideration, prioritization, implementation influence, or another defensible mechanism.

The stronger research act is often to preserve the itch without flattering it.

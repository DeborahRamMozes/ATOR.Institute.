# RUPAISM and CorRUPtruction: A Working Origin of My Own Art Genre and Art Criticism Theory

**Author:** Deborah Ram Mozes  
**Date:** 3 June 2026  
**Section:** Kumpulan Kritik  
**Status:** Working theory, expanded from 2018 thesis incubation into 2026 formulation

## 1. Why I Needed to Build My Own Ground

I created RUPAISM because I could not honestly place my art inside one existing art genre without forcing the work into a container that did not fully fit. I like Cubism. I understand its fracture, its reordering of space, and its refusal of a single perspective. But my work does not fully belong inside Cubism. I also feel close to Surrealism, Brut Art, Outsider Art, figuration, decorative language, symbolic body, and material-based practice. Yet none of those existing categories became the right soil for my art to stand on.

My work touched them, borrowed air from them, and passed near them, but did not root there. It was not enough to call the work surrealist, because the distortion in my work does not only come from dream, fantasy, or unconscious image. It comes from social pressure, institutional hypocrisy, gendered looking, material fragility, personal memory, professional discipline, and cultural signs. It was not enough to call it figurative, because the figure in my work is not only a body. It is a sign, a wound, a structure, a social battlefield, and a constructed visual argument.

It was also not enough to call it decorative, because ornament in my work is not merely surface beauty. Ornament can become pressure, mask, ritual, cultural memory, and symbolic authority. It was not enough to call it outsider or brut, because my work is not outside knowledge. It is built from academic experience, professional skill, design discipline, personal consciousness, taste, failure, laughter, anger, and long artistic practice. The existing labels were useful as references, but they did not become my ground.

So I dared myself to build a theory.

In 2018, during my thesis, I used RUPAISM as my own visual-conceptual framework. It was bold. Maybe too bold for that time. Maybe too strange to be accepted comfortably inside fine art study. When I said it was my own theory, the academic response around me was not fully ready for it. Some people laughed, because institutions often laugh at what they cannot immediately classify. That is their little survival costume. But the theory was already written, my thesis was accepted, and I graduated.

Back then, RUPAISM was still half-baked. It had fire, but not enough kiln time. It had instinct, but not yet enough structure. It had the beginning of a ground, but the ground had not yet hardened into a full method. After 2018, I entered a long period of artistic incubation. From 2018 to 2026, through paper, drawing, cutting, installation, virtual space, body imagery, material exploration, and many forms of personal and professional experience, the theory slowly became stronger. The pieces were placed inside the kiln of my own mind. Now, in 2026, RUPAISM is no longer only a thesis gesture. It can be articulated as a stronger theory, a genre, and an art criticism method.

## 2. RUPAISM as Genre

RUPAISM is a paper-based visual-conceptual genre that treats image, body, material, culture, and space as interconnected structures. It does not begin from beauty as decoration. It begins from the act of reading form as a system of signs. In RUPAISM, the artwork is not only an object to be seen. It is a structure to be read.

The body in RUPAISM is not neutral. The female body especially appears as a social sign, a site of judgment, desire, violence, memory, resistance, and symbolic construction. The figure may be fragmented, distorted, sensual, awkward, layered, cut, repeated, or displaced. This is not visual accident. It is a method of showing how the body is pressured by society, culture, institution, gaze, taste, and power.

Paper in RUPAISM is also not a passive material. Paper is body, skin, archive, wound, surface, fragility, document, ruin, and architecture. It can be drawn on, cut, folded, layered, suspended, framed, enlarged, scanned, or rebuilt in physical and virtual space. Through this process, paper becomes a living structure of thought.

RUPAISM therefore does not stand as a simple style. It is not only about how the artwork looks. It is about how the artwork thinks through material. It creates a specific visual language where drawing, cutting, paper, body, color, shadow, frame, and space operate as signs.

## 3. CorRUPtruction as the Critical Engine

Inside RUPAISM, I develop the theory of CorRUPtruction. The word comes from the relation between **corrupt**, **rupture**, and **construction**.

Corrupt does not only mean legal corruption or government bribery. In this theory, corrupt refers to a wider structural condition. It is the damage, distortion, hypocrisy, bias, gatekeeping, exclusion, and polished decay that I observe in academia, government, the art world, curatorial authority, galleries, society, and cultural systems. A system can look educated, elegant, spiritual, progressive, or professional while still carrying damaged mechanisms underneath.

Rupture is the moment when that polished surface cracks. Rupture happens when the hidden pressure becomes visible. It appears when academic authority mocks what it cannot classify, when curatorial taste becomes gatekeeping, when galleries filter value through market politeness, when government becomes theater, when society reduces women into surfaces, and when cultural institutions expose the violence behind their clean language.

Construction is the act of rebuilding from those broken signs. I do not only complain about corrupted systems. I deconstruct them into material, body, image, line, cut, edge, shadow, color, and space. Then I reconstruct them into a new visual structure.

CorRUPtruction is therefore not destruction for chaos. It is a disciplined reconstruction of corrupted and ruptured reality into raw visual form.

## 4. Deconstruction and Reconstruction

My process often begins from a mental itch, a pressure, an observation, or a rupture in consciousness. I see something around me: academia, government, art world behavior, curator systems, gallery taste, social judgment, gender gaze, or everyday absurdity. Something feels corrupted. Something ruptures. That rupture creates the need to make form.

The work then moves into deconstruction. I break the image, fragment the body, cut the paper, distort the pose, expose the awkwardness, repeat the sign, or isolate the visual pressure. This is not a decorative move. It is a critical operation.

After that, reconstruction begins. The broken parts are layered, stitched, framed, hung, shadowed, enlarged, or spatialized. The final work may look raw, wild, weird, uncomfortable, or aesthetically unpleasing. But this unpleasing quality is intentional. It refuses to decorate damage into polite beauty. It refuses to make rupture look clean just because the art world likes surfaces that behave.

The result is not a failed beauty. It is an unpleasing aesthetic: a visual condition where rawness, discomfort, instability, and fracture become structure.

## 5. Unpleasing Aesthetic

Unpleasing aesthetic is a major part of RUPAISM and CorRUPtruction. It does not mean ugly work. It does not mean careless work. It means the artwork refuses the demand to be smooth, pretty, polite, balanced, and easily consumed.

The world I respond to is not smooth. Academia is not smooth. Government is not smooth. The art world is not smooth. Curatorial authority is not smooth. Gallery systems are not smooth. Gendered looking is not smooth. So the artwork does not need to fake smoothness.

The raw edge, strange body, awkward pose, unstable frame, limited color, cut surface, and layered shadow become evidence. They show that reality is already pressured before it enters the artwork. My work does not corrupt reality. Reality arrives already corrupted. The artist processes it.

## 6. Chromatic Economy in RUPAISM

Color in RUPAISM works through chromatic economy. I often use a limited palette, especially blue, gold, black, and red. This limitation is not poverty of color. It is discipline.

Blue carries psychic pressure, bruised memory, inner coldness, and emotional depth. Gold carries ornament, sacred residue, cultural authority, power, and the seductive mask of beauty. Black becomes the grammar of rupture: line, wound, shadow, pressure, and structural evidence. Red appears as rupture itself: heat, alarm, flesh, anger, exposure, and social wound.

Like woodcut, etching, and linocut traditions, a restricted color system can intensify line, form, texture, cut, and symbolic force. The fewer the colors, the heavier each color must speak. In RUPAISM, color is not used as decoration. It becomes a coded register of body, rupture, memory, power, and reconstruction.

## 7. RUPAISM as Art Criticism Theory

RUPAISM is not only a genre for producing my own artworks. It can also operate as an art criticism theory.

As a genre, it creates works through paper, body, cut, layer, rupture, reconstruction, and unpleasing aesthetic. As criticism, it reads artworks through the relation between corrupt systems, rupture points, deconstruction methods, reconstruction logic, material evidence, color register, and symbolic body.

This means RUPAISM is not only long conceptual writing. It must also be shown through visual study. A theory becomes stronger when it can be mapped visually: through process documentation, cut-line diagrams, material analysis, body-fragment maps, color studies, shadow studies, artwork case studies, and comparison with other artists or systems.

The critical question is not only whether an artwork is beautiful. The stronger question is: what system produced this form? Where is the rupture? What material carries the evidence? What kind of body is being constructed? Why does the work look raw, strange, or uncomfortable? How does the artwork reconstruct damaged reality into visual knowledge?

## 8. From Half-Baked Theory to Kiln-Fired Structure

In 2018, RUPAISM was already alive, but not fully formed. It was half-baked, and that is not shameful. Many theories begin as heat before they become structure. What mattered was that the seed existed. I had already recognized that my art needed its own ground.

From 2018 to 2026, the theory passed through practice. It was not only thought. It was tested through drawing, paper, cutting, installation, virtual space, body imagery, exhibitions, failures, work, memory, and consciousness. It was shaped by personal life, academic pressure, professional experience, taste, anger, humor, cultural knowledge, and the refusal to let existing genres become a cage.

Now the theory can be articulated more clearly. RUPAISM is the broader visual-conceptual ground. CorRUPtruction is the critical engine. Unpleasing aesthetic is the visual condition. Chromatic economy is the color discipline. Paper-body architecture is the material and spatial method. Together, they form a stronger system for making, reading, and criticizing art.

## 9. Working Definition

RUPAISM is a paper-based visual-conceptual genre and art criticism methodology that reads and constructs art through body, material, culture, memory, rupture, reconstruction, and symbolic form. It emerges from the awareness that existing genres such as Cubism, Surrealism, Brut Art, Outsider Art, figuration, and decorative art may touch parts of my practice, but cannot fully contain it.

Through CorRUPtruction, RUPAISM observes corrupted structures in academia, government, art world systems, curatorial authority, galleries, and society. It identifies rupture as the moment when hidden pressure becomes visible, then deconstructs and reconstructs that rupture through paper, drawing, cutting, layering, shadow, color, frame, and space.

RUPAISM does not seek polite beauty. It builds unpleasing aesthetic as a form of visual truth: raw, wild, strange, uncomfortable, but structurally intentional.

## 10. Closing Statement

I did not create RUPAISM because I wanted to decorate my art with a new label. I created it because the available labels did not give my work enough ground. My art needed a soil of its own: a place where paper, body, rupture, memory, cultural sign, professional knowledge, personal consciousness, academic experience, and social criticism could stand together.

If that was too bold in 2018, then let it be bold. The art world often accepts a theory only after it has survived long enough to become inconvenient. RUPAISM has survived inside my practice. It has moved from thesis instinct into visual method. It has been fired longer in the kiln of my own mind. Now it returns as a stronger structure: not perfect, not final, but grounded enough to be studied, tested, criticized, expanded, and used.

This is not only my art genre. It is also my way of reading art, systems, bodies, materials, institutions, and rupture.

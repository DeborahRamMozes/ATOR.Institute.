## Evidence grades

| Grade | Meaning | Use |
|---|---|---|
| A | Primary source, official filing, technical document, standard, or strong dataset | Can support a firm claim if the wording stays inside the source boundary |
| B | Reputable reporting, repeated signal, strong secondary analysis | Useful but should preserve source limits |
| C | Plausible, partial, anonymous, modeled, or incomplete | Carry as caution, not settled fact |
| D | Weak, speculative, unsupported, or too vague | Do not promote without stronger evidence |
| Rejected | Contradicted or unsupported claim | Do not repeat unless new evidence appears |

## Current source discipline

When ATØR records a claim, the ledger should identify:

- date;
- source;
- geography;
- material or infrastructure layer;
- evidence type;
- hypothesis connection;
- risk level;
- what changed;
- what should be rejected or downgraded;
- next watch item.

## Claims to downgrade by default

- “Efficiency improvement means equal reduction in total power or water.” Deployment rebound can erase that simplistic story.
- “A data-center capacity announcement proves actual buildout.” Permits, financing, grid interconnects, and construction remain gates.
- “A lease guarantee is money already spent.” Guarantees are conditional obligations, not immediate cash deployment.
- “A chip price rumor proves LLM price rises.” Pricing pass-through requires direct evidence.
- “A model update proves secret coordination.” Interface timing and product rollout do not prove institutional causation by themselves.
- “AI writing is automated labor replacement.” Serious writing still carries correction, authorship, accountability, taste, and context labor.
